The world produces hundreds of millions of tonnes of plastic each year, roughly half for single-use products such as packaging, straws, and carry bags. A bag used for minutes can persist for centuries, breaking into ever smaller pieces, and enormous amounts reach the ocean, where currents gather floating patches, turtles mistake bags for jellyfish, and whales wash ashore with plastic-filled stomachs. The debris fragments into microplastics found from mountain snow to table salt and even human blood, with long-term health effects still under study. Recycling handles only a small share because plastics melt differently, contamination ruins batches, and disposal is often cheaper. Environmental groups therefore push source reduction: bans and taxes on single-use bags, refill stations, reusable alternatives, and repairable products. Volunteer cleanups remove tonnes of litter and build public pressure for new laws, while individual habits like reusable bags matter once whole neighborhoods adopt them.
