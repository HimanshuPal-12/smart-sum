Millions of children in rural areas finish primary school unable to read, and large learning surveys repeatedly find that more than half of rural pupils in several countries cannot read a grade-two text while studying in grade five. The causes include long walks to distant schools, overcrowded classrooms without electricity or clean water, a chronic shortage of teachers willing to work in isolated postings, and absenteeism that rises during harvest season. There is progress, however: volunteer reading camps, mobile libraries in vans and on bicycles, mother-tongue instruction in the early years, and solar-powered tablets bringing recorded lessons to offline classrooms are slowly improving literacy. Experts stress that no single reform is enough; attendance, supported teachers, local-language books, and community involvement must work together, and literacy gained by children often spreads to parents, shortening the next generation's struggle.
