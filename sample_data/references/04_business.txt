Electronic commerce has shaken small family shops that once lived on neighborhood familiarity, as customers compare prices on their phones and expect doorstep delivery. The pandemic accelerated the shift, and owners discovered that a photo catalogue, a payment link, and a delivery arrangement were enough to begin selling online. Mobile payments attract cashless customers but their fees squeeze thin margins, while social media offers cheap marketing that reaches thousands nearby. Obstacles remain real: single-package delivery is costly without volume, returns can erase profits, large platforms out-discount small sellers, and scarce bank credit without collateral pushes owners to fund growth from savings. Government and industry programs offer digital training, subsidized storefronts, and micro-loans, though awareness is uneven. Analysts note that successful shops rarely compete on price alone but on trust, speed, and personal service — the corner store's original strengths, unchanged by technology.
