Sleep is the most neglected basic health habit, with roughly one in three adults regularly sleeping less than the recommended seven to nine hours. During deep sleep the brain stores memories and clears waste while the body repairs muscles, balances hormones, and strengthens the immune system. Chronic short sleep is linked to higher rates of obesity, high blood pressure, heart disease, and diabetes, slows reaction times like mild intoxication, and worsens anxiety and depression. Most sleep problems respond to simple habits rather than medicine: a consistent bedtime and wake time, a cool, dark, quiet bedroom, no coffee after mid-afternoon, and no screens in the hour before bed, since bright light delays melatonin. Persistent snoring, long sleepless stretches, or daytime exhaustion despite a full night in bed may signal disorders such as sleep apnea and deserves a doctor's attention. Protecting sleep may be the most effective free health decision most people can make.
